## SFSpeechRecognizer: new segmentation

This run measures the SFSpeechRecognizer pipeline rewritten after 0.3.1 ([plan](../plans/sfspeech-segmentation.md)). To compare like with like, MacDub 0.3.1 was run on the same audio, on the same Mac and macOS 27.0, right before:

| SFSpeechRecognizer | 0.3.1 | 0.4.0 |
|---|---|---|
| **Words lost** (deletions) | 14.7 % | **7.1 %** |
| Word error rate | 36.3 % | **22.9 %** |
| · insertions | 6.7 % | 2.1 % |
| **Whole sentences** | 55.4 % | **61.2 %** |
| Sentence ends: recall | 27.1 % | 41.7 % |
| Latency, median · p90 · max | 1.1 · 3.2 · 22.3 s | **1.0 · 2.4 · 8.9 s** |
| Repeated words | 7 | 0 |
| Segments emitted | 617 | 463 |

SpeechAnalyzer's numbers did not change with it (9.8 % WER, 1.3 % lost, 84.9 % whole, 2.3 · 4.1 · 12.4 s, before fast results — next section).

Run again later the same day (the results at the top): 59.4 % whole, 6.8 % lost, 23.8 % WER — SFSpeechRecognizer varies by a couple of points from run to run.

What changed, and why (all measured with `--log-updates` on the first 5 minutes of this audio):

- **SFSpeechRecognizer restarts its transcript inside a task** after a pause ("…upon the scent of some new problems" → "I"), and sometimes sends a revised copy of the previous utterance afterwards. The segmenter lost the pending text and the new start, or spoke the previous utterance again. It now detects both. On the slice, this alone took the words lost from 10.9 to 6.8 %.
- **A rotated run is finished, not cancelled.** Cancelling threw away the audio the task had not transcribed yet. `endAudio()` delivers the missing words ("…was to introduce a") and a final result in about 1 s. The next task must wait for it: a second on-device task cancels the first one (error 301), even on another `SFSpeechRecognizer`. Meanwhile its request buffers the audio, and the task catches up.
- **"Silence" is silence in the audio.** 27 of the 29 rotations on the slice were text stalls in the middle of speech, and they lost the words being said. A text stall alone no longer rotates the run. It cuts the pending text instead and keeps the task (SFSpeechRecognizer stalls mostly at sentence ends, but people may still be talking).
- **A pause in the audio followed by a stall of 0.6 s is a sentence end.** Partial results carry no word timings, so pauses come from the audio level.
- **Provisional punctuation**: a period at the end of a partial result counts once more text follows ("for Irene." becomes "for Irene Adler").
- **Time cuts** wait 8 s instead of 4.5 s. Their clock also restarted wrongly, so they fired every ~2 s. Clause and length cuts come later (160 / 240 characters), because SFSpeechRecognizer punctuates little.
- **The periodic rotation waits for a pause** between 30 and 45 s. Rotating at 30 s instead lost more words: shorter runs give the recognizer less context.
- **Re-anchoring** after a revision aligns words (a dropped comma no longer yields "hich has accomplished"). A character anchor could match the same words ending an earlier sentence and repeat it.

What is left of the lost words is the recognizer itself. Of the words missing on the 5-minute slice, the pipeline drops fewer than 1 %: the rest never appear in any partial result.

## Update: SpeechAnalyzer with fast results

Live translation needed SpeechAnalyzer's `.fastResults` reporting option: without it, a conversation (a sentence, then silence) was processed in blocks of about 12 s of audio. The results at the top of this report use it (the whole report was run again on the same Mac and audio after the change). The first run of the day, without it:

The translated voice starts three times sooner with as many whole sentences, for half a point of word error rate. MacDub uses it for dubbing too since this measurement; `--no-fast-results` runs the benchmark without it.

## Method

**Audio.** `scripts/benchmark/make-audio.py` takes the opening of *The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes* (Project Gutenberg #1661, public domain) and has macOS `say` read it one sentence at a time, three English voices taking turns by paragraph (Samantha, Reed and Shelley when installed). Sentences are joined with 0.35 s pauses (0.9 s between paragraphs). Because the audio is built sentence by sentence, the reference knows exactly what was said and when each sentence ends. The text has dialogue, questions and long sentences, which is what makes punctuation and segmentation measurable. Synthesized speech is cleaner than a real video, so absolute error rates are optimistic; the comparison between engines is what matters.

**Pipeline.** MacDub runs headless (`MacDub --benchmark-recognition <file> --engine <analyzer|legacy>`): the file is converted to what the capture engines deliver (mono float32, 48 kHz, 1024-frame buffers) and fed to the real `SpeechAndTranslationManager` **in real time**, so run rotation, silence cuts and segmentation behave exactly as while dubbing. Every emitted segment — the unit that gets translated and spoken — is recorded with the audio time it came out at. Translation and speech synthesis are not part of the measurement.

**Engines.**
- **SpeechAnalyzer** (macOS 26+): `SpeechTranscriber` with volatile and fast results (fast since 2026-09-24; `--no-fast-results` measures without them). MacDub segments its *finalized* results — whole, corrected sentences — and only shows the volatile ones live.
- **SpeechAnalyzer (volatile)** (`--analyzer-volatile`, optional): the older behaviour, segmenting volatile results as they arrive.
- **SFSpeechRecognizer**: on-device recognition with partial results. Runs are rotated at the first pause in the audio after 30 s (at 45 s at the latest) and on real silence; a rotated run finishes its audio instead of being cancelled.

**Metrics** (`scripts/benchmark/compare.py`), after aligning recognized words to the reference (case and punctuation ignored):
- *Word error rate*: (substitutions + deletions + insertions) / reference words.
- *Whole sentences*: share of reference sentences whose recognized words all came out in one segment. A sentence split over several segments is translated and spoken in pieces, and the voice sounds cut.
- *Punctuation*: precision and recall of sentence ends (`. ? !`) and commas after correctly recognized words, and how many questions end in `?`.
- *Latency*: from the end of a sentence in the audio to the emission of the segment holding its last recognized word.
- *Stability*: the same metrics per 10-minute window, repeated text, gaps.
- *Resources*: MacDub's CPU and memory (sampled in-process every 5 s) and the `localspeechrecognition` XPC service serving the run (`top` every 5 s). `top`'s energy column on Apple Silicon tracks CPU for these processes; Neural Engine energy is not measured (that needs `sudo powermetrics`).

**Run your own** on any Mac (Apple Silicon recommended; SpeechAnalyzer needs macOS 26):

```bash
make benchmark                          # 30 min of audio per engine, one engine at a time
scripts/benchmark/run.sh --minutes 10   # quicker
scripts/benchmark/run.sh --volatile     # also measure SpeechAnalyzer's volatile mode
```

The first run asks for Speech Recognition permission for MacDub. Keep the Mac awake and plugged in, and avoid heavy work while it runs. The report lands in `docs/benchmarks/<date>-<chip>.md` — pull requests with results from other chips are welcome.
