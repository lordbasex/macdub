## Notes

This is the first benchmark of MacDub's recognition pipeline. It was run while fixing the SpeechAnalyzer path, and the earlier runs of that night are what drove the fixes:

| SpeechAnalyzer | Whole sentences | Word error rate | Sentence-end recall | Commas recall | Questions | Latency median · p90 · max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volatile results segmented (MacDub ≤ 0.2.0) | 35.2 % | 11.1 % | 85.5 % | 43.0 % | 13/26 | 1.68 · 3.03 · 3.8 s |
| Finalized results only | 93.3 % | 9.5 % | 90.4 % | 85.3 % | 21/27 | 2.30 · 3.86 · 19.9 s |
| Finalized + completed-sentence fallback after 5 s (this release) | 92.3 % | 9.7 % | 91.1 % | 83.9 % | 19/26 | 2.31 · 4.06 · 19.6 s |

What the runs found, all fixed in this release:

- **Crash** (`EXC_BREAKPOINT` in `SpeechRecognizerWorker.preRunRecognition`): audio reached SpeechAnalyzer before its preferred format was known and went in unconverted. Audio is now held until the format is known.
- **Doubled fragments** ("the speecheech analyzer", "async streamream") and **words cut mid-way** ("predomin", "the ab"): SpeechAnalyzer's *volatile* results contain them; its *final* result for the same audio is a whole, corrected, punctuated sentence. MacDub now segments finals only and shows volatile text live.
- **Sentences split in pieces**: SpeechAnalyzer reports text in bursts every 3–4 s, and the pipeline took each pause between bursts for a pause in speech. For it, a silence cut now needs quiet *audio*.
- **Late sentences**: SpeechAnalyzer sometimes holds a final back until a very long sentence ends (one in the text is 467 characters), taking the short sentences before it along, up to 20 s late. After 5 s without a final, sentences the volatile text has already completed go out anyway: on three of the four slowest stretches the worst delay went from 13–20 s to 3–8 s. It cannot help when the volatile text has no sentence end yet, so an occasional ~20 s wait remains (the *Latency, max* row); median and p90 are unaffected.

The SFSpeechRecognizer figures in *Results* come from the same script; with it, MacDub still cuts sentences on silence and rotates runs every 45 s, as before.

On real audio (a WWDC session played in Chrome, dubbed English → Spanish on this Mac) the finals-only pipeline delivered one complete sentence per segment, e.g. *"In my record function, which I call as the user presses record, I'm going to request audio permission and start the AVAudio session."*

The first two rows come from an earlier 31-minute cut of the same text and voices; the third is this report's run (28 minutes), so compare them as trends rather than to the decimal.

## Update: latency cap (MacDub 0.3.1)

The run above has one sentence spoken 19.6 s after it ended: SpeechAnalyzer merged it with the next one (its volatile text had a comma where the period belonged) and only finalized both 13–20 s later. 0.3.1 bounds that wait: after 10 s without a final, the volatile text goes out up to its last clause mark. Same audio, SpeechAnalyzer only:

| | No cap (0.3.0) | Clause cut after 7 s | Clause cut after 10 s (0.3.1) | Cut at audio pauses after 6 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sentences spoken whole | 92.3 % | 71.8 % | 84.9 % | 88.5 % |
| Word error rate | 9.7 % | 9.9 % | 9.8 % | 9.8 % |
| Sentence ends found (recall) | 91.1 % | 90.0 % | 90.0 % | 92.4 % |
| Latency, median | 2.3 s | 2.3 s | 2.3 s | 2.3 s |
| Latency, p90 | 4.1 s | 4.1 s | 4.1 s | 4.1 s |
| Latency, max | 19.6 s | 12.4 s | 12.4 s | 15.1 s |
| Longest gap between segments | 31.7 s | 11.5 s | 15.2 s | 31.7 s |

Cutting sooner (7 s) splits long sentences that were going to arrive whole; cutting where the audio pauses (the speaker's real sentence end — volatile results carry one time range for their whole text, so the pause is matched to a clause mark by position) measured no better than the 10 s clause cut. Run-to-run variation in SpeechAnalyzer's finals is about ±3 points on the whole-sentence share.

## Method

**Audio.** `scripts/benchmark/make-audio.py` takes the opening of *The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes* (Project Gutenberg #1661, public domain) and has macOS `say` read it one sentence at a time, three English voices taking turns by paragraph (Samantha, Reed and Shelley when installed). Sentences are joined with 0.35 s pauses (0.9 s between paragraphs). Because the audio is built sentence by sentence, the reference knows exactly what was said and when each sentence ends. The text has dialogue, questions and long sentences, which is what makes punctuation and segmentation measurable. Synthesized speech is cleaner than a real video, so absolute error rates are optimistic; the comparison between engines is what matters.

**Pipeline.** MacDub runs headless (`MacDub --benchmark-recognition <file> --engine <analyzer|legacy>`): the file is converted to what the capture engines deliver (mono float32, 48 kHz, 1024-frame buffers) and fed to the real `SpeechAndTranslationManager` **in real time**, so run rotation, silence cuts and segmentation behave exactly as while dubbing. Every emitted segment — the unit that gets translated and spoken — is recorded with the audio time it came out at. Translation and speech synthesis are not part of the measurement.

**Engines.**
- **SpeechAnalyzer** (macOS 26+): `SpeechTranscriber` with volatile results. MacDub segments its *finalized* results — whole, corrected sentences — and only shows the volatile ones live.
- **SpeechAnalyzer (volatile)** (`--analyzer-volatile`, optional): the older behaviour, segmenting volatile results as they arrive.
- **SFSpeechRecognizer**: on-device recognition with partial results, rotated every 45 s and on silence.

**Metrics** (`scripts/benchmark/compare.py`), after aligning recognized words to the reference (case and punctuation ignored):
- *Word error rate*: (substitutions + deletions + insertions) / reference words.
- *Whole sentences*: share of reference sentences whose recognized words all came out in one segment. A sentence split over several segments is translated and spoken in pieces, and the voice sounds cut.
- *Punctuation*: precision and recall of sentence ends (`. ? !`) and commas after correctly recognized words, and how many questions end in `?`.
- *Latency*: from the end of a sentence in the audio to the emission of the segment holding its last recognized word.
- *Stability*: the same metrics per 10-minute window, repeated text, gaps.
- *Resources*: MacDub's CPU and memory (sampled in-process every 5 s) and the `localspeechrecognition` XPC service serving the run (`top` every 5 s). `top`'s energy column on Apple Silicon tracks CPU for these processes; Neural Engine energy is not measured (that needs `sudo powermetrics`).

**Run your own** on any Mac (Apple Silicon recommended; SpeechAnalyzer needs macOS 26):

```bash
make benchmark                          # 30 min of audio per engine, one engine at a time
scripts/benchmark/run.sh --minutes 10   # quicker
scripts/benchmark/run.sh --volatile     # also measure SpeechAnalyzer's volatile mode
```

The first run asks for Speech Recognition permission for MacDub. Keep the Mac awake and plugged in, and avoid heavy work while it runs. The report lands in `docs/benchmarks/<date>-<chip>.md` — pull requests with results from other chips are welcome.
